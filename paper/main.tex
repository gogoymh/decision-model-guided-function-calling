\begin{abstract}
Function calling requires choosing a function and arguments, or withholding a call. We present \textbf{Decision-Model-Guided Function Calling (DMFC)}, which combines schema validation with binary function and argument judgments. Rejected candidates and actual feedback accumulate before the unchanged request; repair never executes functions. With one decision model and at most three repairs, three-run BFCL accuracy rises from 64.1\% to 74.8\% with GPT-5-nano and 81.1\% to 82.1\% with GPT-5.4-mini, but falls from 88.3\% to 87.0\% with Claude. On an exploratory 156-case API-Bank first-call adaptation, accuracy rises from 60.0\% to 66.5\%, 78.2\% to 79.5\%, and 86.3\% to 87.2\%, respectively. A single-run BFCL ablation finds a 1.1-point advantage over latest-only feedback. Effects vary across generators and require additional inference time and cost. API-Bank's input was refined after exploratory outcomes; this evaluation measures call prediction, not execution success.
\end{abstract}
